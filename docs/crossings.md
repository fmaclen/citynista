# Footpath crossings

Where a footpath crosses a car road the drawing paints a crossing: a zebra over car lanes and a refuge over a median. A crossing is paint. No node or cut marks it, the connector offers nothing of it, and a footpath drawn, pasted or moved across a car road changes no node or segment of either. This page covers how a crossing is found, how it is drawn, where the traffic meeting it is held, and how its control is stored and carried through edits.

## Where a footpath crosses (`core/runs.ts`)

A crossing is read one way, off the drawing alone.

- **The roadway** is the car road as drawn (`Roadway`): the whole road a segment is part of, carried on through the nodes it only carries on through, along the middle of its car lanes as they show, as wide as they are.
- A walkway down the street's middle parts the roadway into one for each carriageway (`carriagewaysOf` in `core/attach.ts`). Each is crossed on its own and reaches up to that walkway, a buffer beside it included, so a crossing paves all between the walkways it joins: zebra over the car lanes and refuge over the rest. A footpath ended on that walkway crosses the carriageway it came over, and one right across crosses each and walks the middle as the walkway it is.
- Past an end where other car roads meet it, the roadway runs on into their junction: round its end node as far as it is wide, and inside the roads meeting there. Past a dead end it runs on not at all, so a footpath walked round a dead end crosses nothing.
- **The footpath** is read where its paving lies: where it shows, shift and all, and at a node a bend takes it round, along the bend's middle to halfway round (`roundOf` in `core/footpaths.ts`, `drawnLine` here). So a bend turning back inside the roadway never left it.
- Where a point lies across the road is read the way the drawing lays the lanes (`acrossRoad` in `core/road-line.ts`): the point of the drawn line it lies square to, and how far right of the shown line it lies along that line's normal, the shift there included. A point on the curb is on it, not inside the road. Which end of the road a point lies past is read square to the drawn line unshifted, its curve kept, on its first or last sampled chord (`alongPath` on `drawn` in `core/runs.ts`).
- **A run** is a way along the footpath pieces inside one roadway (`stretchesOf`), joined at the footpath nodes inside it, passing no node twice, from one curb to the other. A run needs no node of its own, so a footpath dragged across a roadway shows its crossing on every frame.
- The pieces and nodes on some way across are found at once (`onWays`: the edges of one biconnected block holding an edge that joins the two curbs), whatever the size, ids or order of the graph.
- The footpath pieces inside one roadway joined at nodes there are one crossing, held by the least of their segments (`crossingsHeldBy`). Pieces not so joined are crossings apart: a footpath that leaves a roadway and comes back crosses it twice.
- A run crossing the road's middle inside the junction at the road's end is read as crossing the road there it crosses most squarely, alone (`heldAt`). Ids and order never decide it. Where that node is a junction rather than a node of two sides, the run is no crossing at all (below).
- So a footpath ended on a street stops at its curb. One dipping into the roadway, or bent back at a node in it, and out on the side it entered, is not drawn over the lanes and is given way to by no one.

### A crossing lies on road ground alone

A crossing is one route of footpath segments from curb to curb, the run or runs `crossingsHeldBy` finds. It lies on road ground alone, or it is none: it paints nothing, holds no traffic, shows no badge and reads no control stored for it, and the car roads under it are drawn exactly as if the footpath were not there. It is none where **its route's own paving runs on onto a junction's ground** (a node where car roads meet, merged with others or not): the strips of its segments and the joints at their nodes, read over the car ground they lie on, in one piece from the ground it crosses to a junction's. Car ground within twice `SEAM` (2 cm) is one ground across the gap, whichever pieces it belongs to, so pieces drawn edge to edge are one, and closing that gap moves no curb: a footpath that steps off the asphalt onto a sidewalk between parts one piece from the next. Two roads' asphalt that close is touching to the eye, and is read as touching.

A junction paints no crossing and clears nothing for one (`drawJunction` in `road-model/node.ts`): it is drawn exactly as in the same city without footpaths, but for what the approach of a crossed road puts down there to hold traffic for a crossing on that road, and a footpath's own paving over its verges. So a crossing whose paving stays on its road but whose band tips over the road's mouth is painted on the road up to the junction's edge, and no further.

Only the route counts. Another footpath joined to it, a branch leaving the route's node and running into the junction, leaves the crossing as it is and makes none of its own there: the crossing is what an owner expects over a plain road. Two things of a branch do count, because they are the route's own. The joint at the route's node is the route's paving, so a branch that leaves a node close enough to the mouth for that joint to reach the junction takes the crossing with it. And a branch that gives a second way from curb to curb is found as one crossing with the route (`crossingsHeldBy`), all of whose paving is read. A node of two sides (a bend) is road, so every crossing at a bend is read as above. A junction runs its own traffic and has its own crosswalks on its arms, so a path through it walks over the asphalt with nothing painted, its own paving stopping at the curb each side.

This is decided in one place, `onJunction` in `core/networks.ts`, for each crossing `crossingsHeldBy` finds. It reads only the crossing's own ground and the car pieces whose boxes meet its route's paving, so whatever reads the crossing again reads the decision again, and the editor's drawing never differs from a fresh build's. So a crosswalk across an arm stands as long as its route's paving there stays on the arm, and goes otherwise, its paint and its lines with it. With none painted on a junction's ground, a crossing holds no approach but its own road's.

## How a crossing is drawn

- Each run's ground is its band (`bandOf` in `core/runs.ts`): the footpath's own width carried straight from its edges at one curb to its edges at the other, bent only as the road bends between them. A footpath crossing a curved street at a shallow angle is one band following the street.
- The band reaches on past each curb along the footpath, so the road's ground cuts it where the footpath's edges meet the curb. Where the footpath runs straight across the curb the two meet edge to edge, however slant; the band reaches no further along the curb than 20 widths.
- A crossing is laid on the pieces of its own road, the roads meeting it at its ends, and their junctions alone (`Crossed.on`, `crossedBy` in `road-model/overlay.ts`). A road merged away is its junction's. A junction paints none of it (above), though its ground counts towards the crossing's stripe rhythm.
- The car piece draws it (`crossingsOver`): stripes along the traffic, a metre apart and centred on what it crosses, one at the least. All its pieces share one rhythm, so a crossing over a road and its node lines up.
- A car lane the rhythm crosses less than half a stripe wide gets a stripe of its own across its middle: every car lane crossed carries stripe. A crossing too small to draw is none.
- The car piece's lines and hatching stop under the crossing. Painting changes no ground: a median keeps the nose it has with nothing laid over it.
- Nothing else of a car road reads a footpath.

## Where traffic is held (`road-model/holds.ts`)

Every car approach a crossing lies on is held by the crossing's control: give-way teeth, a stop line for a stop or a signal, nothing for none. Where each line stands, which lanes it lies across and what paint it clears are decided once, in `holds.ts`, for each crossing and each stream of traffic meeting it, from the whole crossing and the whole way the traffic drives (roads, nodes of two sides and junctions alike). The road, the node and the junction only paint their share (`Share`): the lines falling on their ground and the cleared ground there, for every kind of paint they lay. No piece decides whether a line exists or where. The rules are stated in the comment at the top of `holds.ts` and above `approachPlans`; in short:

- **A stream** is the car lanes of the carriageway the crossing lies across running one way (`holdsGoing`), with the paths across nodes of two sides that join them. A crossing holds only the car lanes of its own carriageway (`holdsLane`): on a street with a walkway down its middle each carriageway reads its own crossing's control.
- The stream meets the crossing where its first lane does, read along the traffic. Its line stands `signBack` behind that, square to the traffic, across every lane of the stream whose traffic meets the crossing, on whichever piece that falls.
- The paint of the stream's whole carriageway (the lanes between the walkways either side, both directions) is cleared from the line up to the crossing, and no further than the crossing reaches across the stream's lanes at a slant (`shortOf`). Across a node of two sides, whose carriageway is read off both roads it joins, it is cleared along each path up to where that path meets the crossing (`shortAlong`). A junction's ground is never cleared for a crossing: its lines carry on to its edge. A line standing for several crossings clears up to all of them.
- The line needs `signRoom` behind it, on ground the traffic drives (not past where its road leaves a junction, nor off a free end), clear of every other crossing on the stream's lanes. A piece's own crossing, which has no control (a street's crosswalk, a junction's walkway, a sidewalk a node carries across), takes the line away only where it lies under the line's own paint; between the line and the footpath, overlapping it or beyond it, it leaves the line alone. Where that room is missing the line is left out: the whole marking lies on the road or is not drawn.
- Two crossings are two crossings, each with its own line, unless their ground on the stream's lanes overlaps or leaves no room for a line between them. Then the one the traffic meets first stands its line for both; of two met at once, the one holding all the stream's lanes.

### At a junction's approach (`approachPlans`)

- On one approach, in driving order, come its arrows, one line, the crossings near the junction, and the junction.
- A crossing over the approach's lanes whose near side lies within the approach's stop room (short of where its arrows' tails would stand with no crossing there), and any crossing too close behind such a one for a line between, is the approach's own where the approach has room behind it for a line. Its line stands behind the last of them, and no stream stands another line for them.
- Each lane coming in is marked on that one line by the strongest of the junction's control and those of the crossings holding that lane, a signal the strongest. Lanes marked alike share one stretch of line, parted at a walkway down the middle where different crossings hold the two sides. The junction's control alone, with no crossing there, runs one line across the whole road, walkway and all, as a car road alone draws it.
- The arrows stand behind that line, and behind any crossing further back that would fall on them and its line.
- `approachesOf` in `road-model/approach.ts` draws the approach from its plan: badge, line and arrows. The plan places the rows (`ApproachPlan.rows` in `road-model/holds.ts`). `endCut` hands the road where its paint stops: the plan's cleared share for the lanes the line holds, and where it would stop with nothing laid over the node for the rest (`EndAsk.held`).

`checks.ts` holds every drawing to this: no marking of one approach lies on another or on a crossing, no paint of any kind lies on a crossing, no second line for the same traffic and no lane line's end lies between a line and the crossing it stands for. `unit/crossing-lines.test.ts` sweeps arrangements for the other half, that every lane meeting a crossing with room for a line has exactly one.

## Crossing controls (`core/crossing-controls.ts`)

A crossing's control is give-way unless one is set on it by hand. All that is stored of a crossing is such a control. The comment at the top of `crossing-controls.ts` is the full rule; its parts:

- **Stored on the footpath.** An entry is kept on a footpath segment (`SegmentData.controls`) for one carriageway it crosses: the car road by that road's segments as it carries on (`roads`: every segment it runs on through, round a loop once, `chainThrough` in `core/place.ts`), and the carriageway by the ids of its car lanes (`lanes`). Where a loop is parted into two roads for drawing (`roadThrough`) is a matter of drawing alone: the user never made a parting and cannot see it, and a cut, a bend, a setback, a side street or a paste moves it, so nothing stored or kept from one edit to the next reads a road as parted. A loop is one road to its controls, as it was before loops were parted. Nothing is stored on a car road, so no footpath edit writes a car segment; a car road read with entries drops them, and a footpath made a car road loses them.
- An entry without `lanes` (cities saved before carriageways were told apart) names every carriageway of its road, the same on every load. An entry naming a junction's nodes and no road (`nodes`, written for crossings over junctions by an editor that drew them) names nothing: it is read and kept as stored, and dropped by the next edit saved (`Networks.settle`), as any entry no crossing lies on is.
- **One control per footpath segment and carriageway.** Every crossing that segment makes of that carriageway takes it: a footpath bowed across one carriageway twice, or one straight path across a loop's top and bottom, holds both crossings to one control, set from either. Nothing tells crossings apart by order or place along the footpath, so no edit moves a control from one crossing to another. A user who wants two such crossings to differ splits the footpath between them.
- A segment's control for a carriageway is the strongest of its entries naming it, give-way where none does (`controlOf`). A crossing over pieces of several footpath segments takes the strongest of theirs, and setting it writes it on each of them.
- **Carried through car edits.** Whatever replaces a car segment (a cut, a draw's cut, a join, `Graph.setLanes` for a lane edit or lanes pasted) carries each entry naming it by the car lanes it names (`carryControls`). The entry goes to the carriageways of the replacement now holding any of those lanes, wherever they lie: two carriageways merged take both entries (the stronger reads), one split gives both halves its entry. An entry none of whose lanes stands on the replacement goes; it is never handed to another carriageway.
- Lanes pasted, none of them the road's own whatever their ids, go by place instead: each old carriageway's entry to the new one counted the same from the road's edge, if any.
- An entry always names lanes of each road it names. A carry that would give its roads different lanes writes one entry per set.
- **Carried through footpath edits.** A paste keeps entries on the copy (`layPaste` in `clipboard.ts`, lane ids copied). A footpath cut gives both pieces every entry. A join gives the joined footpath both pieces' entries (`joinedControls`), the stronger where they differ.
- **Rewritten only on a saved edit.** As part of an edit the user saves, the editor writes each footpath's entries as its crossings now stand (`Networks.settle`, `Editor.keepCrossingControls`, `settled`): one per carriageway it crosses whose control is other than give-way, naming its road's segments and car lanes now. An entry no crossing of its segment lies on is dropped and never comes back on a crossing made later. Old entries are written in this shape. Selecting, opening or loading writes nothing, and a save that changes nothing records nothing.
- Rewriting changes no crossing's control, so nothing is drawn again for it.

## Crossing badges

The badge on a crossing steps its control through none, stop, yield and signal, as an approach's badge does in the connector, one undo step a click (`Editor.cycleCrossing`).

- In select mode a badge sits on every crossing a selected road is part of or lies across, each crossing once, kept while the selection holds (`Networks.crossingsOn`, reading the crossings the last build filed by segment). Only crossings drawn over car lanes or a median have one; a crossing wholly covered by a wider one is drawn nowhere and has none.
- A double-click anywhere on a zebra opens its badge alone, changing nothing (`openCrossing`), over the ring of a footpath node in it too; only a car node's ring opens its connector first. Its first click selected the road and so put a badge under the second, which then opens rather than steps; a double-click on a badge already shown steps it twice (`pressCrossing` in `components/ControlBadges.svelte`).
- An open badge stays on its crossing through edits (`Networks.sameCrossing`). Crossing ids are numbered along the footpath, so an id may pass to another crossing as crossings come and go. The badge follows the crossing drawn now across the same carriageway whose footpath pieces lap the old one's, the one lapping furthest, and closes once none does. Its carriageway is carried through car edits as an entry is (`Graph.carrying`), so a lane edit, a paste, a cut or a join leaves the badge where its control stays.
- Badges never stand on one another on screen, at any zoom: they keep one size on screen while the map grows and shrinks under them, so they are laid out where they are shown, every frame (`placeBadges` in `components/badge-layout.ts`), for every badge shown, the connector's approach badges included. A badge stands on its crossing's middle where it is clear of those placed before it, otherwise at the nearest clear point round its middle. A badge that would show in the map's view at its crossing's middle, wholly or in part, stands wholly within the view: on the nearest point inside, moved from there only to a clear point inside, and with none, on that point; one well out of the view stays out of it. A badge moved off its middle is tied to it by a short line (`ControlBadges.svelte`). `crossingBadges` holds each crossing's middle, not where its badge shows.
- `__editorLook()` reports the open badge (`crossing`) and every badge shown (`crossings`).

## What is cached

- `Networks` keeps each footpath segment's crossings while all four of these hold (the comment above the loop in `Networks.build` is the authority):
  - No node they read lies on a footpath piece drawn afresh or gone, or near a car piece that came or went. The nodes read are those of every footpath piece joined to the segment inside a roadway.
  - No car piece drawn afresh or gone has a box, as it is or as it was, that meets a crossing's band box. The band boxes are filed in a `BoxGrid`, and a crossing is cut from those pieces only. One of them may lie clear of the footpath's paving, such as a junction beside the road's mouth.
  - The car pieces a crossing is laid on, which its holds are shared to, stay the same. These are the roads and junctions round its road's ends, and they are checked only where one of them is drawn afresh or gone. A piece joins that list only where one already on it changes, so an edit away from the crossing's road and its ends reads no crossing again.
  - Its road as it carries on, which its control names, stays the same. It is checked only where a car piece of that road is drawn afresh or gone (`chainOf` in `core/runs.ts`, once per piece): a loop is drawn as two pieces, so an edit of one half reads the crossings on the other again only where it changes the loop's segments.
- A crossing's footpaths given new controls rebuild that crossing alone: only those footpaths' control lists are compared, by identity.
- The ground crossed is filed in a `BoxGrid` as it comes and goes.
- `Drawing` keeps each crossing's decision and each junction's plan from paint to paint (`Drawing.decisions`), each behind what it read: the roads and nodes on its way, the junction plans, and the crossings near the stretch of lane its room covers. Each painted piece records the crossings it was painted with, so when a crossing comes, changes or goes, every piece it is or was drawn on has its share read again.
- `Drawing.paint` reads what is laid over a car piece again only where the piece is new or crossed ground came, went or changed near it. A changed control is changed ground, so it rebuilds its crossing and repaints the car pieces under it.
- Holding traffic for a crossing reads the road within reach of it alone, however long the road or however many segments it is joined from: the lanes and node paths a stream's legs run along (`laneNear`, `wayNear` in `holds.ts`), the stretch of roadway it lies over (`stretchOver`), and points placed on the roadway (`project` and `pieceAt` in `road-model/geometry.ts`). The ground it is cut from, the roadway it is placed on and painting it read the road whole; [road-model.md](road-model.md) lists what is read whole and why.
